Yuka Saso became one of the LPGA Tour's breakout stars with a powerful swing and a calm temperament. Here are 20 things to know about her.

  1. She was born in the Philippines. Saso was born in 2001 in Bulacan province, north of Manila.
  2. Her heritage is Filipino and Japanese. Her mother is Filipina and her father is Japanese.
  3. She has represented both countries. She played for the Philippines early in her career, including at the Olympics, and later took Japanese citizenship.
  4. She chose Japanese citizenship at 21. Japanese law requires dual nationals to choose one nationality, and she chose Japan.
  5. Her father pushed her training. He helped build her early fitness and practice routine, which included plenty of strength and conditioning work.
  6. She modelled her swing on Rory McIlroy's. She has said she studied videos of McIlroy to develop her own powerful action.
  7. She turned professional in 2019. She joined the professional ranks in Japan after her amateur success.
  8. She won Asian Games gold. At the 2018 Asian Games in Indonesia, she won the women's individual gold and helped the Philippines to team gold.
  9. She won quickly in Japan. After turning pro, she won twice on the JLPGA Tour in her rookie season.
  10. She won the 2021 U.S. Women's Open. She beat Nasa Hataoka in a playoff at the Olympic Club in San Francisco.
  11. She tied a record for youngest winner. At 19 years, 11 months and 17 days, she matched Inbee Park as the youngest U.S. Women's Open champion.
  12. She was the first Filipino major winner. That 2021 win made her the first golfer from the Philippines to win a major.
  13. She came back from a rough start. At the Olympic Club she recovered from two early double bogeys in the final round to force the playoff.
  14. She played the Tokyo Olympics. She represented the Philippines at the 2020 Games, held in 2021.
  15. She won a second U.S. Women's Open. In 2024 she won again at Lancaster Country Club in Pennsylvania.
  16. She did it for Japan the second time. Her 2024 victory came as a Japanese citizen, so she has won the U.S. Women's Open representing two countries.
  17. She hits it a long way. She is consistently among the longer hitters in women's golf.
  18. She is bilingual on the global stage. She gives interviews in both English and Japanese.
  19. She stays calm under pressure. Commentators often point to her composure in big moments as one of her greatest strengths.
  20. She is still young. She won two majors before turning 23, so her best years may still be ahead.
