Professional golf has dozens of tournaments every season, but four stand above the rest. The men's major championships carry the most prestige and history, and they are how players' careers are judged. Winning all four in a career, the career Grand Slam, has been achieved by only a handful of players.

1. The Masters (April)

Venue: Augusta National Golf Club, Georgia, USA (every year)

The Masters is the only major played at the same course every year, and that makes Augusta National the most familiar course in golf. Its traditions are famous:

  • The winner receives a green jacket.
  • The defending champion hosts the Champions Dinner and chooses the menu.
  • The Par 3 Contest is held on Wednesday.
  • The stretch of holes 11 to 13 is known as Amen Corner.

Jack Nicklaus holds the record with six Masters wins. Tiger Woods has five.

2. The PGA Championship (May)

Venue: rotates between courses in the United States

The PGA Championship is run by the PGA of America, and its field traditionally includes a group of club professionals alongside the world's best players. The winner lifts the Wanamaker Trophy, one of the largest trophies in sport. Walter Hagen and Jack Nicklaus share the record with five wins each.

3. The U.S. Open (June)

Venue: rotates between courses in the United States

The USGA runs the U.S. Open, which is known as golf's toughest test. Narrow fairways, thick rough and firm, fast greens often push the winning score close to par. Anyone with a low enough handicap can try to qualify, which gives it a uniquely open spirit. Willie Anderson, Bobby Jones, Ben Hogan and Jack Nicklaus share the record of four wins.

4. The Open Championship (July)

Venue: rotates between links courses in the United Kingdom

First played in 1860, The Open is the oldest major. It is played on seaside links courses, including St Andrews, Royal Birkdale, Carnoustie and Royal Portrush, where wind and weather are a big part of the challenge. The champion receives the Claret Jug and the title "Champion Golfer of the Year". Harry Vardon holds the record with six wins.

What about the women's majors?

Women's professional golf has five majors: the Chevron Championship, the U.S. Women's Open, the KPMG Women's PGA Championship, the Evian Championship and the AIG Women's Open. Each has its own history and roll of great champions.

Why the majors matter

Prize money, rankings and tour wins all count, but golf history is written at the majors. When fans debate the greatest players of all time, they almost always start by counting major titles.
