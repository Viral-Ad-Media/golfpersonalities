Golf has a vocabulary all its own, and much of it is very funny. Learn these terms and you'll fit right in, both on the course and at the 19th hole.

Shots

  • Worm burner: a shot that never leaves the ground and skims along the grass.
  • Duck hook: a shot that hooks hard and dives to the left (for a right-hander) soon after it's hit.
  • Banana ball: a big slice that curves sharply to the right.
  • Chili dip: a chip where the club hits the ground first and the ball barely moves.
  • Shank: a shot struck off the hosel that shoots off at a wild angle. Many golfers won't even say the word.
  • Skull: hitting the top half of the ball so it screams low across the green.
  • Grip it and rip it: swinging as hard as you can without worrying where it goes.

Scores

  • Snowman: a score of 8 on a hole, because the number looks like a snowman.
  • Sandy: making par after being in a bunker.
  • Up and down: getting the ball in the hole in two shots from off the green.
  • Army golf: "left, right, left, right": hitting it all over the course.

Lies and situations

  • Fried egg: a ball half-buried in a bunker with sand around it like an egg white.
  • Texas wedge: using your putter from off the green.
  • Gimme: a short putt your playing partner lets you pick up without holing (in casual play and match play only).
  • Dance floor: the putting green.
  • In the jail: stuck behind trees with no clear shot.

Players and behaviour

  • Sandbagger: someone who keeps their handicap high on purpose to win bets and competitions.
  • Hacker / duffer: an affectionate term for a weak or occasional golfer.
  • Breakfast ball: a free do-over on the first tee in casual rounds.
  • Mulligan: a do-over shot, not allowed under the Rules of Golf but common among friends.
  • The yips: nervous twitches that ruin short putts.

Next time someone calls your eight a "snowman" or your putter from the fringe a "Texas wedge", you'll know exactly what they mean.
