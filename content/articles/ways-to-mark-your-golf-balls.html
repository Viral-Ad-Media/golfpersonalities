Picture this: you and your playing partner both hit the same brand and number, and both balls finish in the same patch of rough. Which one is yours? Marking your ball solves the problem, and it can help your game too.

Why mark your golf ball?

  • Identification: under the Rules of Golf, you're responsible for playing the right ball. Playing a wrong ball in stroke play costs a two-stroke penalty. The rules recommend putting an identifying mark on your ball.
  • Lost balls: you have three minutes to search for a ball, and a clear mark makes finding and confirming yours quicker.
  • Alignment: a line on the ball can help you aim putts and tee shots.
  • Personality: a custom mark makes your ball unmistakably yours.

Ways to mark your ball

1. Dots or initials

The simplest method is a permanent marker. A few coloured dots in a pattern, or your initials, make your ball easy to identify. Tour players often use a signature pattern of dots.

2. An alignment line

Draw a straight line around the ball, or along its logo, and use it to aim on the green. Inexpensive line-drawing tools make this quick and accurate.

3. Stencil templates

Plastic stencils let you draw shapes, symbols and logos neatly on the ball. They're great for a consistent, professional-looking mark.

4. Personalised balls

Many manufacturers and retailers offer custom printing with initials, names or logos. Personalised balls are popular as gifts.

5. Coloured balls

Brightly coloured balls are easier to spot. Add a simple mark too, though, since others may use the same colour.

Marking your ball on the green

"Marking" also means something else: placing a ball marker behind your ball on the putting green before you lift it to clean it or move it out of someone's line. Under the Rules of Golf you must mark the spot before lifting your ball on the green.

  • Use a flat ball marker or a coin.
  • Place it directly behind the ball, then lift the ball.
  • If your marker is in another player's line, move it one or more putter-head lengths to the side and remember to move it back before you putt.

Tips

  • Use a permanent, quick-drying marker so the ink doesn't smudge.
  • Tell your playing partners your mark on the first tee.
  • Keep spare marked balls in your bag so you're always ready.
