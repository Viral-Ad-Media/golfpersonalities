When you start playing golf, the clubs get all the attention. But a handful of inexpensive accessories will make your rounds more comfortable, more enjoyable and more in keeping with golf etiquette. Here's what's worth buying first.

10 worth buying

  1. Golf balls: beginners lose balls, so skip premium tour balls at first. Choose an affordable, durable two-piece ball and buy plenty.
  2. Tees: keep a mix of long tees for the driver and short tees for irons and hybrids.
  3. A golf glove: it improves your grip and prevents blisters. Most right-handed golfers wear one on the left hand.
  4. A divot repair tool: fixing pitch marks on the green is basic etiquette, and every golfer should carry one.
  5. Ball markers: you need something to mark your ball on the green. Many divot tools include a magnetic marker.
  6. A towel: clean clubs and balls perform better. Clip it to your bag.
  7. Golf shoes: spiked or spikeless golf shoes give you grip during the swing and comfort over a long walk.
  8. A golf bag: a lightweight stand bag suits beginners who walk. If you ride in a cart, choose a cart bag.
  9. Weather gear: a waterproof jacket, an umbrella and a cap or visor make bad-weather rounds bearable.
  10. Sun protection: you'll spend four hours or more outside, so pack sunscreen and water.

Nice to have later

  • Rangefinder or GPS: great for learning your distances, but not essential in your first few rounds.
  • Push cart: worth it once you're walking 18 holes regularly.
  • Headcovers: protect your driver and woods from scratches.
  • Training aids: alignment sticks and a putting mat are the best value.

Skip for now

You don't need expensive tour balls, high-end launch monitors or a bag full of gadgets to enjoy golf. Spend on lessons instead: a few sessions with a qualified coach will do more for your game than any accessory.
