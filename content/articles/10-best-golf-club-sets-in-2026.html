A complete set of golf clubs takes the guesswork out of building a bag. You get a driver, fairway wood or hybrid, irons, wedge, putter and usually a bag, all designed to work together. Here are 10 options to consider, grouped by the type of golfer they suit. Models are refreshed often, so check the current version before you buy.

Best for complete beginners

1. Callaway Strata

The Strata line has been a go-to beginner set for years. It's forgiving and lightweight, it comes with a bag, and it's priced for people who are just starting out.

2. Wilson Ultra and Profile sets

Wilson's complete sets offer good value and plenty of forgiveness, which makes them popular for new and occasional golfers. The Profile line includes higher-spec options for players who want a step up.

Best for improving players

3. TaylorMade RBZ SpeedLite

The RBZ SpeedLite package brings design ideas from TaylorMade's mainstream clubs into a complete set. Its lightweight build helps golfers generate speed.

4. Cobra Fly-XL

Cobra's complete set is built around game-improvement technology. Its forgiving irons and hybrids are easy to launch.

5. A premium box set

Direct-to-consumer brands now sell premium complete sets with better components than traditional starter sets. They're worth a look if you want quality without buying club by club.

Best for specific golfers

6. Women's complete sets

Most major brands make women's sets with lighter shafts, shorter lengths and higher lofts. These features help produce a better launch at moderate swing speeds.

7. Junior sets

Children need clubs sized to their height. Junior sets are sold by age or height range and include fewer, lighter clubs so youngsters can swing freely.

8. Senior sets

Sets aimed at senior golfers focus on light weight, flexible shafts and hybrids in place of long irons. They help golfers with slower swing speeds keep their distance.

9. Left-handed sets

Most complete sets now come in left-handed versions, but stock can be limited. Order early if you're a lefty.

Best for committed golfers

10. Build your own set with a fitting

Once you're playing regularly, a custom fitting is worth the investment. Pairing forgiving irons with a fitted driver and wedges gives you clubs matched to your swing.

What to look for in any set

  • Shaft flex: regular flex suits most beginners. Senior or ladies flex suits slower swings.
  • Hybrids rather than long irons: they're much easier to hit.
  • Driver loft: beginners usually benefit from 10.5 degrees or more.
  • The bag: stand bags suit walkers, cart bags suit golfers who ride or use a trolley.
  • Number of clubs: you don't need the full 14 to start. A smaller set can be easier to learn with.
