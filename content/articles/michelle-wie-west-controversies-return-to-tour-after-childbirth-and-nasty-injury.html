Few golfers have lived as much of their career in public as Michelle Wie West. She was a household name before she could drive a car, she won a major championship, and she dealt with a run of injuries that would have ended many careers earlier. Along the way she was at the centre of several debates.

A teenage phenomenon

Born in Honolulu in 1989, Wie became famous at an age when most golfers are still playing junior events. At 13 she won the U.S. Women's Amateur Public Links, and as a teenager she played in LPGA majors and contended in them. She also accepted invitations to play in men's professional events, including the PGA Tour's Sony Open in Hawaii.

The debates that followed her

  • Playing against men: her starts in men's events divided opinion. Supporters saw a talented teenager testing herself. Critics argued she should first learn to win against women. She never made a cut in a PGA Tour event, and the debate lasted for years.
  • Her professional debut: she turned pro in 2005, shortly before her 16th birthday. In her first event as a professional, the Samsung World Championship, she was disqualified for an improper drop that a reporter flagged after the round. It was a harsh introduction to life as a pro.
  • Withdrawals and pressure: in 2007 she withdrew from events through injury, and some withdrawals were criticised at the time. Big sponsorship deals had brought very high expectations very early.
  • Speaking up: in 2021 she publicly criticised demeaning comments Rudy Giuliani made about her on a podcast. Many people in golf backed her.

Proving herself: Stanford and a U.S. Women's Open

Wie studied at Stanford University while playing professionally and graduated in 2012. Two years later came the peak of her career: she won the 2014 U.S. Women's Open at Pinehurst No. 2. She finished with five LPGA Tour victories.

The injuries

Her body did not make things easy. She dealt with wrist, hand, back and neck problems for much of her career. In 2018 and 2019 she struggled with her right hand and wrist, had surgery, and stepped away from competition to recover.

Marriage, motherhood and a comeback

In 2019 she married Jonnie West, a Golden State Warriors executive and son of basketball great Jerry West. Their daughter, Makenna, was born in June 2020. In 2021, about a year after giving birth, Wie West returned to the LPGA Tour. She talked openly about the challenge of rebuilding her game and her fitness as a new mother, and about the many hours she spent working on it.

Stepping away

She stepped back from full-time competition in 2022 and played her final U.S. Women's Open at Pebble Beach in 2023. She has since remained a prominent voice for women's golf.

Michelle Wie West's career wasn't the straight line many predicted when she was 13. Still, its mix of controversy, setbacks and real achievement made her one of the most influential women in modern golf.
