If you've watched golf on television, you've heard it: a soft, restrained round of applause after a good shot or a tidy putt. That's the golf clap, and it says a lot about the culture of the game.

The meaning

A golf clap is quiet, polite applause, usually a few gentle claps with the fingers rather than the full palms. It shows appreciation without being loud.

Why golf fans clap quietly

Golf needs concentration. At a tournament, several players may be about to hit shots nearby, and a loud noise at the wrong moment can put someone off. Quiet applause lets fans recognise a good shot without disturbing anyone else. This respect for the players is a big part of golf etiquette.

The golf clap isn't the only sound you'll hear, though. A holed bunker shot, an eagle or a winning putt brings huge roars at big events. At places like the Ryder Cup or the 16th hole at the Phoenix Open, crowds are famously loud.

The sarcastic golf clap

Off the course, the "golf clap" has a second meaning. Away from the golf course, slow, quiet applause can be sarcastic. It suggests something wasn't very impressive, or mocks someone who made a mistake. Among friends, a gentle golf clap after a poor shot is a classic bit of teasing.

Golf clap etiquette

  • Wait until the ball has finished moving before you clap.
  • Stay still and quiet while any player nearby is preparing to hit.
  • Applaud good shots from every player, not just your favourite.
  • Save the big cheers for the big moments.

The golf clap is a small habit, but it shows something about the game: good manners, respect for opponents and appreciation for skill.
