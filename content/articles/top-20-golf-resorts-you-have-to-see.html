Some golf trips are about the course, some are about the scenery and the best ones are about both. Here are 20 golf resorts and destinations that belong on every golfer's bucket list. Always check current availability and booking rules before you plan a trip.

United States

  1. Pebble Beach Resorts, California: Pebble Beach Golf Links runs along the cliffs of the Monterey Peninsula and has hosted many U.S. Opens.
  2. Pinehurst Resort, North Carolina: often called the "Cradle of American Golf". Its famous No. 2 course was designed by Donald Ross.
  3. Bandon Dunes Golf Resort, Oregon: walking-only links-style golf on the Pacific coast, with several highly ranked courses.
  4. Kiawah Island Golf Resort, South Carolina: home of the Ocean Course, which hosted the 1991 Ryder Cup and the 2012 and 2021 PGA Championships.
  5. TPC Sawgrass, Florida: home of the Players Championship and the island-green 17th.
  6. Destination Kohler, Wisconsin: includes Whistling Straits, which hosted the 2021 Ryder Cup on the shores of Lake Michigan.
  7. The Broadmoor, Colorado: a historic mountain resort with a long tradition of championship golf.
  8. Streamsong Resort, Florida: dramatic courses built on former phosphate mining land.
  9. Sea Island, Georgia: coastal golf and the home of the PGA Tour's RSM Classic.

Scotland, Ireland and Wales

  1. St Andrews, Scotland: the "Home of Golf". Anyone can apply to play the Old Course through the public ballot.
  2. Gleneagles, Scotland: a grand Perthshire hotel with championship courses, host of the 2014 Ryder Cup.
  3. Turnberry, Scotland: the Ailsa course and its lighthouse are among the most photographed sights in golf.
  4. Adare Manor, Ireland: a luxury estate in County Limerick, chosen to host the 2027 Ryder Cup.
  5. Celtic Manor, Wales: host of the 2010 Ryder Cup.

Rest of the world

  1. Cabot Cape Breton, Canada: dramatic links and clifftop golf in Nova Scotia.
  2. Casa de Campo, Dominican Republic: home of Pete Dye's Teeth of the Dog, with holes right along the Caribbean.
  3. Cape Kidnappers, New Zealand: clifftop holes high above the Pacific Ocean.
  4. Fancourt, South Africa: a Garden Route resort with several acclaimed courses.
  5. Sun City, South Africa: home of the Gary Player Country Club and the Nedbank Golf Challenge.
  6. Monte Rei, Portugal: a Jack Nicklaus signature course in the Algarve, one of Europe's top golf regions.

Tips for planning a golf trip

  • Book tee times early, especially at famous courses with limited public access.
  • Check whether caddies are required or recommended. At many top courses they are part of the experience.
  • Pack for the weather. Links golf in Scotland and Ireland can bring four seasons in a day.
  • Leave time to enjoy the 19th hole as well.
