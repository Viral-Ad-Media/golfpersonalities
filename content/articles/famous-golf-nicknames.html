Golf has a long tradition of colourful nicknames. Some come from a player's style, some from childhood and some from a moment that fans never forgot. Here are 10 of the most famous.

1. The Golden Bear: Jack Nicklaus

Nicklaus's blond hair and powerful build earned him the "Golden Bear" nickname early in his career. He won a record 18 professional majors, and the golden bear became his logo and brand.

2. The King: Arnold Palmer

Palmer's charisma and attacking style brought golf to a television audience in the 1950s and 60s. His loyal fans were known as "Arnie's Army", and he became simply "The King".

3. Tiger: Eldrick Woods

"Tiger" is so familiar that many people forget it's a nickname. His father, Earl Woods, named him after a fellow soldier and friend from the Vietnam War, Vuong Dang Phong, whom Earl called "Tiger".

4. The Big Easy: Ernie Els

Els's large frame and smooth, unhurried swing made "The Big Easy" a perfect fit. The South African won four majors and made the game look effortless.

5. Lefty: Phil Mickelson

Mickelson is right-handed in daily life but plays golf left-handed, so "Lefty" was a natural nickname. He won six majors as one of the most successful left-handed golfers ever.

6. The Shark: Greg Norman

The Australian earned "The Great White Shark" for his aggressive play, his blond hair and his love of deep-sea fishing. He spent many weeks as world No. 1 and won two Open Championships.

7. The Black Knight: Gary Player

Player often dressed all in black, which inspired his nickname. The South African won nine majors and completed the career Grand Slam.

8. Super Mex: Lee Trevino

Trevino rose from humble beginnings in Texas to win six majors. He was known as "Super Mex" and "The Merry Mex" for his Mexican-American heritage and his non-stop humour on the course.

9. Slammin' Sammy: Sam Snead

Snead's long, powerful swing earned him "Slammin' Sammy". He holds the record for PGA Tour wins, shared with Tiger Woods.

10. El Niño: Sergio Garcia

Garcia burst onto the scene as a teenager and was nicknamed "El Niño", Spanish for "the kid". He finished runner-up to Tiger Woods at the 1999 PGA Championship and finally won his first major at the 2017 Masters.

Honourable mentions

  • The Hawk / Bantam Ben: Ben Hogan
  • Boom Boom: Fred Couples
  • The Squire: Gene Sarazen
  • The Walrus: Craig Stadler
