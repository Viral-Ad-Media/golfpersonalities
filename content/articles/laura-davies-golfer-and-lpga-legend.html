Few players have meant as much to women's golf as Laura Davies. She hit the ball a long way, played fast and won all over the world for more than three decades. She became the face of European women's golf and one of the best players the game has seen.

Early years

Davies was born in Coventry, England, in 1963 and grew up in Surrey. She was largely self-taught, built her game on natural power, and turned professional in 1985. In her first seasons on the Ladies European Tour she topped the Order of Merit, an early sign of what was to come.

Major champion

Davies won four major championships:

  • 1987 U.S. Women's Open: she won in a playoff, which earned her LPGA Tour membership.
  • 1994 LPGA Championship
  • 1996 LPGA Championship
  • 1996 du Maurier Classic

In 1994 she became the first European player to reach No. 1 in the women's world rankings of the time.

Winning everywhere

Davies won 20 times on the LPGA Tour and more than 40 times on the Ladies European Tour, with further wins in Asia and Australia. Her total of more than 80 professional titles worldwide is one of the best records in the women's game.

A Solheim Cup stalwart

She played in the first 12 editions of the Solheim Cup, starting in 1990. She was a key part of Europe's early wins against the United States.

Honours

Davies was inducted into the World Golf Hall of Fame, and in 2014 she was made a Dame (DBE) for services to golf. In 2018, aged 54, she won the inaugural U.S. Senior Women's Open by a wide margin, which showed her game had lasted well beyond her peak years.

Personality

Fans have always loved Davies for her personality as well as her trophies. She was famous for playing quickly, hitting driver almost everywhere and following plenty of other sports. She has also worked as a television commentator and kept competing into her 50s and 60s.

Legacy

Laura Davies showed a generation of European girls that they could take on and beat the best players in the world. Her mix of power, longevity and joy in competition makes her a true legend of the LPGA and of English sport.
