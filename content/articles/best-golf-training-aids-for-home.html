You don't need a driving range to get better at golf. A few well-chosen training aids can help you practise at home in short sessions that fit around everyday life. The best ones have something in common: they give you clear feedback about what your club and body are doing.

1. A putting mat

Putting accounts for a large share of every round, and it's the easiest skill to practise indoors. Choose a mat with a realistic roll and a target cup. Some include alignment lines and slopes. Even ten minutes a day can sharpen your stroke.

2. Alignment sticks

They're cheap, simple and used by tour professionals. Alignment sticks help you check your aim, ball position and swing plane. Lay one along your target line and one for your feet, and you'll quickly spot alignment faults.

3. A practice net

If you have a garden or garage, a net lets you hit full shots safely. Pair it with a hitting mat to protect the floor or lawn.

4. An impact bag

An impact bag is a padded bag you swing into. It teaches you to arrive at impact with your hands ahead of the ball and a solid body position. Because the ball doesn't fly, it's ideal for small spaces.

5. A swing mirror

A mirror gives instant visual feedback on posture, grip and positions during the swing. Many golfers use one to check their setup before every session.

6. A tempo or weighted trainer

Flexible-shaft and weighted trainers help you feel smooth rhythm and sequence, and they double as a warm-up tool before you play.

7. A personal launch monitor

Portable launch monitors have become much more affordable. With a net, they show ball speed, launch and estimated carry distance. Some connect to simulator software so you can play virtual rounds.

Making home practice work

  • Practise little and often: 15 focused minutes beats an occasional two-hour session.
  • Work on one thing at a time: pick a single goal for each session.
  • Film your swing: your phone is a powerful training aid.
  • Make it a game: set putting challenges, like holing 10 in a row from three feet.
