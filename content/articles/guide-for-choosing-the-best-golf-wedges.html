Wedges are among the most important clubs in the bag. They handle approach shots, pitches, chips and bunker shots, the shots that most decide your score. Choosing the right ones comes down to a few key ideas.

The four types of wedge

  • Pitching wedge (PW): usually around 43 to 47 degrees. It normally comes with your iron set and is used for full shots and longer chips.
  • Gap wedge (GW or AW): around 48 to 52 degrees. It fills the yardage "gap" between the pitching and sand wedge.
  • Sand wedge (SW): around 54 to 56 degrees. It's built for bunkers but very useful around the green.
  • Lob wedge (LW): around 58 to 62 degrees. It's for high, soft shots over hazards and onto fast greens.

1. Get your gapping right

Modern iron sets often have strong lofts, so your pitching wedge may be just 43 or 44 degrees. Check its loft first, then build your wedges in steps of about 4 to 6 degrees. A common setup is 46, 50, 54 and 58 degrees. Even gaps give you predictable distances for full swings.

2. Understand bounce

Bounce is the angle between the leading edge and the lowest point of the sole. It stops the club from digging.

  • Low bounce (4 to 6 degrees): suits firm turf, tight lies and players with a shallow swing.
  • Mid bounce (7 to 10 degrees): the most versatile option for most golfers and conditions.
  • High bounce (11 degrees or more): suits soft turf, fluffy sand and players with a steep swing who take big divots.

3. Consider the grind

A grind is extra shaping of the sole, usually at the heel and toe. It lets the club sit lower when you open the face for creative shots. Skilled players use grinds to fine-tune performance. Beginners usually do best with a standard, all-round sole.

4. Choose blade or cavity back

Traditional blade-style wedges give better players more feel and control. Cavity-back wedges add forgiveness for mid- and high-handicap golfers, so a mishit still finishes closer to the target.

5. Check the grooves

Grooves create spin, especially from the rough and on short shots. They wear down with use. If you practise a lot, you may need to replace your most-used wedge every couple of seasons to keep spin consistent.

6. Get fitted if you can

A short wedge fitting, or even an hour on a launch monitor, can show your distance for each loft and how your swing interacts with the turf. That's the most reliable way to choose loft and bounce.

Quick recommendations

  • Beginners: pitching wedge plus a 56-degree sand wedge with mid-to-high bounce.
  • Improving players: add a gap wedge to cover the distance between them.
  • Low handicappers: three or four wedges with fitted lofts, bounce and grinds.
