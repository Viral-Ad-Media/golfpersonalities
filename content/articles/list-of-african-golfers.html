For a continent with relatively few golf courses compared with Europe or North America, Africa has produced an extraordinary number of champions. South Africa and Zimbabwe especially have punched far above their weight, with players who have won majors, topped the world rankings and inspired new generations.

Gary Player (South Africa)

"The Black Knight" is one of the greatest golfers of all time. Player won nine major championships and is one of only a handful of men to complete the career Grand Slam. He was known for his fitness and his worldwide travel, and he also won major titles on the senior circuit. He became one of golf's great ambassadors.

Bobby Locke (South Africa)

Locke was famous for his putting, which many believe was among the best ever. He won The Open Championship four times between 1949 and 1957 and had considerable success in the United States in the late 1940s.

Ernie Els (South Africa)

"The Big Easy" won four majors: the U.S. Open in 1994 and 1997 and The Open in 2002 and 2012. His smooth, effortless swing is one of the most admired in the game. He reached world No. 1 and has been a long-time supporter of autism charities.

Retief Goosen (South Africa)

Goosen was quiet and steady, and he won the U.S. Open twice, in 2001 and 2004. He was a regular in the world's top 10 during the 2000s and was later inducted into the World Golf Hall of Fame.

Nick Price (Zimbabwe)

Price won three majors: the 1992 PGA Championship, the 1994 Open Championship and the 1994 PGA Championship. He was world No. 1 in the mid-1990s and is widely respected as one of the game's true gentlemen.

Trevor Immelman (South Africa)

Immelman won the 2008 Masters, finishing ahead of Tiger Woods. He later became a respected television analyst.

Louis Oosthuizen (South Africa)

Oosthuizen has one of the purest swings in golf. He won the 2010 Open Championship at St Andrews by seven shots and has finished runner-up in all four majors.

Charl Schwartzel (South Africa)

Schwartzel won the 2011 Masters in memorable style by birdieing the final four holes. He has also had a long, successful career on the European Tour.

Ashleigh Buhai (South Africa)

Buhai won the 2022 AIG Women's Open at Muirfield after a playoff. It was the biggest win of her career and a landmark moment for South African women's golf.

Other names to know

  • Mark McNulty (Zimbabwe): a prolific winner on the European Tour and in southern Africa.
  • Tim Clark (South Africa): winner of the 2010 Players Championship.
  • Branden Grace (South Africa): shot the first 62 in men's major championship history at the 2017 Open.
  • Lee-Anne Pace (South Africa): a multiple winner on the Ladies European Tour and an LPGA Tour champion.

A lasting tradition

From Bobby Locke's putting to Ernie Els's rhythm, African golfers have shaped the way the game is played. With strong amateur programmes and the Sunshine Tour giving young players a path to professional golf, the tradition looks set to continue.
