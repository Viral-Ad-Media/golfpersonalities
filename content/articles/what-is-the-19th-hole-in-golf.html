Ask a golfer where they're heading after the round and there's a good chance the answer is "the 19th hole". There's no extra hole to play, though. The 19th hole is golf slang for the clubhouse bar or restaurant where players gather after their round.

What happens at the 19th hole?

The 19th hole is where the social side of golf happens. Players:

  • Add up scorecards and settle friendly bets
  • Buy a round of drinks, often paid for by the losers of a match
  • Relive the best shots (and laugh about the worst ones)
  • Grab a meal before heading home

For many golfers, especially in club golf, it's as much part of the day as the round itself.

Why does golf have 18 holes?

The "19th" only makes sense because a standard round has 18 holes, and that standard comes from St Andrews in Scotland. Early golf courses had different numbers of holes. In 1764, the Old Course at St Andrews was changed from 22 holes to 18 by combining several short holes. As St Andrews became the most influential club in the game, 18 holes became the norm around the world.

You may hear a popular myth that 18 holes is how long a bottle of whisky lasts at one shot per hole. It's a fun story for the 19th hole, but there's no evidence for it.

Other meanings of the 19th hole

  • Extra holes: when a match-play contest is tied after 18 holes, the first extra hole is sometimes called the 19th.
  • Novelty holes: a few courses have built a real "19th hole", usually a short bonus par-3 for settling bets.
  • Bar and restaurant names: plenty of golf-themed bars around the world are simply called "The 19th Hole".

19th hole etiquette

A few unwritten rules keep things friendly. Shake hands on the 18th green before you head in. Honour your bets. If someone made a hole-in-one, tradition in many clubs says they buy the drinks.
